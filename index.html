Computational Philosophy

I view machine learning not merely as function approximation, but as an empirical laboratory for discovering structure. Whether that manifests as uncovering the boundary between topological spin liquid states with 2D Continuous Wavelet Transforms, or preventing hallucination in high-stakes clinical retrieval harnesses, the underlying mandate remains identical: clarity over spurious correlation.

My background combines rigorous computational analytics with deep technical immersion in PyTorch, Julia, and distributed systems. I care as much about numerical stability and reproducible seeds as I do about clean APIs and human-centered design.
